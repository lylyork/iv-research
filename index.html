Under the guidance of Professor Yifan Hu, I study statistical inference and budgeted data acquisition for online instrumental-variable regression.

My starting point was statistical inference for the TOSG-IVaR and OTSG-IVaR algorithms of Chen et al. (2024), including asymptotic normality and online covariance estimation for their averaged iterates. These results led me to a design question: how should observations be acquired to improve estimation precision under a fixed expected budget? Drawing on the importance-sampling perspective of Aolaritei et al. (2026), I formulated a budgeted acquisition problem, derived its optimal policy, and developed online methods for learning acquisition policies from streaming data.

01 / Background

Preliminaries: the IV model and OTSG-IVaR

Chen et al. (2024) formulate streaming instrumental-variable regression as a conditional stochastic-optimization problem and propose TOSG-IVaR and OTSG-IVaR. In the one-sample setting used by OTSG, each arriving observation is \(W_t=(Z_t,X_t,Y_t)\), where \(Z_t\in\mathbb R^{d_z}\) is an instrument, \(X_t\in\mathbb R^{d_x}\) is an endogenous regressor, and \(Y_t\) is the outcome. The linear model is

\[\begin{aligned} Y&=X^\top\theta_*+\epsilon_1, &\mathbb E[Z\epsilon_1]&=0,\\ X&=\gamma_*^\top Z+\epsilon_2, &\mathbb E[\epsilon_2\mid Z]&=0. \end{aligned}\]

The errors may be dependent, so ordinary regression need not identify \(\theta_*\). The first stage estimates \(\mathbb E[X\mid Z]=\gamma_*^\top Z\); the second uses this instrument-predictable component to estimate the structural parameter. Define \(\Sigma_Z=\mathbb E[ZZ^\top]\) and \(A=\gamma_*^\top\Sigma_Z\gamma_*\succ0\).

In this linear setting, the population second-stage objective can be written, up to an additive term independent of \(\theta\), as

\[\begin{gathered} \min_\theta f(\theta)=\frac12\mathbb E[(Y-Z^\top\gamma_*\theta)^2],\\ \nabla f(\theta)=A(\theta-\theta_*). \end{gathered}\]

OTSG-IVaR: learning both stages from one observation

Their one-sample algorithm, OTSG-IVaR, uses one tuple \((Z_t,X_t,Y_t)\) per arrival and jointly updates the first-stage and structural estimates:

\[\begin{aligned} \theta_{t+1}&=\theta_t-\alpha_{t+1}\gamma_t^\top Z_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t\theta_t-Y_t),\\ \gamma_{t+1}&=\gamma_t-\beta_{t+1}Z_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t-X_t^\top). \end{aligned}\]

Both updates use the parameter estimates available before arrival \(t\).

Chen et al.’s two-sample counterpart, TOSG-IVaR, uses the update \(\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\alpha_{t+1}(X_t^\top\theta_t-Y_t)X_t'\). It requires an additional regressor draw \(X_t'\) with the same conditional law as \(X_t\) given \(Z_t\), independent of \((X_t,Y_t)\) conditional on that instrument.

I focus on OTSG because its observation requirements fit the acquisition setting: \(Z_t\) is observed first, and the policy decides whether to acquire \((X_t,Y_t)\). Each acquired pair supports both stages of the update.

02 / My first extension

Statistical inference and online covariance estimation

To quantify uncertainty in OTSG estimates, I study the Polyak–Ruppert average \(\bar\theta_T=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T\theta_t\).

Asymptotic normality and first-stage cancellation

Under the report’s moment and step-size conditions, the averaged estimator is asymptotically normal:

\[\sqrt T(\bar\theta_T-\theta_*)\Rightarrow N\!\left(0,\;A^{-1}\mathbb E\!\left[\epsilon_1^2\gamma_*^\top ZZ^\top\gamma_*\right]A^{-1}\right).\]

The key cancellation. I show that the leading contribution of first-stage estimation error cancels the corresponding component of the second-stage noise. The resulting influence function depends on the structural residual \(\epsilon_1=Y-X^\top\theta_*\).

Consistent online covariance estimator

Let \(V\) denote the limiting covariance above. With \(h_t=\gamma_t^\top Z_t\) and \(\widehat\epsilon_t=Y_t-X_t^\top\theta_t\), the online covariance estimator uses

\[ \widehat A_T=\gamma_T^\top\left(\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^TZ_tZ_t^\top\right)\gamma_T, \qquad \widehat S_T=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\widehat\epsilon_t^{\,2}h_th_t^\top. \]

Replacing eigenvalues of \(\widehat A_T\) below a fixed \(0<\tau<\lambda_{\min}(A)\) with \(\tau\) gives \(\widetilde A_T\). The resulting covariance estimator is consistent:

\[\widehat V_T=\widetilde A_T^{-1}\widehat S_T\widetilde A_T^{-1}\xrightarrow{p}V.\]

For each coordinate \(j\) with \(V_{jj}>0\), this yields an asymptotic \(1-q\) confidence interval \(\bar\theta_{T,j}\ \pm\ z_{1-q/2}\sqrt{\frac{(\widehat V_T)_{jj}}{T}}\).

Analogous asymptotic normality and online covariance estimation results hold for TOSG.

03 / A new design question

Acquisition under a budget: the problem and its ideal solution

In the acquisition setting, \(Z_t\) is observed first. A binary decision \(D_t\) determines whether \((X_t,Y_t)\) is acquired, with \(D_t=1\) denoting acquisition. The policy draws \(D_t\mid Z_t\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(a(Z_t))\), with \(D_t\perp(X_t,Y_t)\mid Z_t\).

To preserve the expected updates of the original OTSG algorithm under selective acquisition, I use inverse-probability weighting. With the current iterates held fixed, let \(g(W_t)\) denote the stochastic gradient in either OTSG update; then \(\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{D_t}{a(Z_t)}g(W_t)\right]=\mathbb E[g(W)]\).

The OTSG influence function \(\phi(W)=A^{-1}\gamma_*^\top Z\epsilon_1\) links acquisition to estimation precision. For a fixed policy \(a\), the asymptotic covariance per raw arrival is \(V(a)=\mathbb E[\phi(W)\phi(W)^\top/a(Z)]\). A nonzero matrix \(M\succeq0\) specifies the parameter directions of interest, with \(M=I\) as the standard choice. Writing \(m_M(z)=\mathbb E[\phi(W)^\top M\phi(W)\mid Z=z]\), I formulate the budgeted acquisition problem:

\[\begin{array}{ll} \displaystyle\min_a & \displaystyle J_M(a)=\operatorname{tr}\{MV(a)\}=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{m_M(Z)}{a(Z)}\right]\\[6pt] \text{s.t.}&\delta\le a(Z)\le1,\quad\mathbb E[a(Z)]=\rho,\quad 0<\delta<\rho<1. \end{array}\]

Optimal acquisition with known population information

When \(m_M(z)\) and \(P_Z\) are known, the budgeted acquisition problem can be solved directly. For a positive, integrable score, the optimal policy is

\[ a_M^*(z)=\operatorname{clip}_{[\delta,1]}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{m_M(z)}{\lambda_M}}\right), \]

where \(\lambda_M>0\) is chosen to satisfy the population budget \(\mathbb E[a_M^*(Z)]=\rho\). Clipping enforces the probability bounds \([\delta,1]\).

04 / Online learning and theory

Online algorithms and their theoretical guarantees

The budgeted acquisition problem optimizes over a function, and its solution depends on unknown population quantities. To make online learning tractable, I restrict the policy space to two finite-dimensional classes: policies that are constant within fixed strata, and policies in a fixed parametric family.

Let \(a_t\) denote the acquisition policy available before arrival \(t\). After observing \(Z_t\), the algorithm acquires \((X_t,Y_t)\) with probability \(a_t(Z_t)\), applies inverse-probability weighting to both IV updates, and updates the policy:

\[\begin{aligned} \theta_{t+1}&=\theta_t-\alpha_{t+1}\frac{D_t}{a_t(Z_t)}\gamma_t^\top Z_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t\theta_t-Y_t),\\ \gamma_{t+1}&=\gamma_t-\beta_{t+1}\frac{D_t}{a_t(Z_t)}Z_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t-X_t^\top),\\[5pt] a_t&\longrightarrow a_{t+1}\qquad\text{(policy update)}. \end{aligned}\]

Direct acquisition over fixed finite strata

For a fixed partition \(C_1,\ldots,C_K\), let the policy take a constant value \(a_j\) on each cell. Writing \(p_j=P(Z\in C_j)\) and \(m_{M,j}=\mathbb E[\phi(W)^\top M\phi(W)\mid Z\in C_j]\), the budgeted acquisition problem reduces to

\[\begin{array}{ll} \displaystyle\min_{a_1,\ldots,a_K}&\displaystyle J_M(a)=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{m_M(Z)}{a(Z)}\right]=\sum_{j=1}^K\frac{p_jm_{M,j}}{a_j},\\[6pt] \text{s.t.}&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^Kp_ja_j=\rho,\qquad\delta\le a_j\le1. \end{array}\]

Let \(a_M^{\mathrm{str}}\) denote the policy that minimizes this criterion over the fixed-stratum class. At time \(t\), the accumulated observations provide estimates \(\widehat p_{t,j}\) and \(\widehat m_{t,j}\). Substituting these estimates into the problem gives the next acquisition policy, \(a_{t+1}(z)=a_{t+1,j}=\operatorname{clip}_{[\delta,1]}\sqrt{\widehat m_{t,j}/\lambda_t}\) for \(z\in C_j\).

Here, \(\lambda_t>0\) is chosen to satisfy \(\sum_{j=1}^K\widehat p_{t,j}a_{t+1,j}=\rho\), the budget under the estimated cell probabilities. Solving this finite-dimensional problem with the current estimates provides the policy update.

Approximating the unrestricted optimum. Consider a deterministic sequence of successively refined finite partitions. Within expanding cubes that eventually cover the instrument space, the maximum cell diameter tends to zero. Under the score conditions in Section 03, with \(M\), the budget, and the probability bounds fixed, their population-optimal policies \(a_{M,r}^{\mathrm{str}}\) satisfy

\[a_{M,r}^{\mathrm{str}}\xrightarrow{L^p}a_M^*,\qquad J_M(a_{M,r}^{\mathrm{str}})\downarrow J_M(a_M^*),\qquad 1\le p<\infty.\]

SGD within a feasible policy family

The second approach uses a fixed family \(a(z;\eta)\), \(\eta\in\mathcal K\), whose members satisfy the probability bounds and population budget. Within this family, the optimal parameter is \(\eta_M^\star=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\eta\in\mathcal K}J_M\{a(\cdot;\eta)\}\), giving the policy \(a_M^{\mathrm{par}}(z)=a(z;\eta_M^\star)\).

For an acquired observation, the quadratic influence score is estimated by \(\widehat q_t=\widehat\epsilon_t^{\,2}\|M^{1/2}\widetilde A_{t-1}^{-1}\gamma_t^\top Z_t\|^2\), where \(\widehat\epsilon_t=Y_t-X_t^\top\theta_t\). The projected SGD update is

\[ \eta_{t+1}=\Pi_{\mathcal K}(\eta_t-s_{t+1}\widehat g_t),\qquad\text{where}\quad \widehat g_t=-\frac{D_t\widehat q_t}{a(Z_t;\eta_t)^3}\nabla_\eta a(Z_t;\eta_t). \]

The updated parameter gives the next policy \(a_{t+1}(z)=a(z;\eta_{t+1})\).

Convergence and statistical inference

Let \(a_\infty\) denote the optimum in the chosen policy class: \(a_M^{\mathrm{str}}\) for fixed-stratum acquisition and \(a_M^{\mathrm{par}}\) for parametric acquisition. Under the conditions stated in the report for each method, I establish policy convergence, IV consistency, and asymptotic normality:

\[\begin{aligned} &(\theta_t,\gamma_t)\longrightarrow(\theta_*,\gamma_*)\quad\text{a.s.},\\[5pt] &\sqrt n(\bar\theta_n-\theta_*)\Rightarrow N(0,V(a_\infty)). \end{aligned}\]

The first-stage cancellation extends to adaptive acquisition. A consistent online covariance estimator uses squared acquisition weights:

\[\begin{aligned} \widehat S_n^{\mathrm{acq}}&=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\frac{D_t}{a_t(Z_t)^2}\widehat\epsilon_t^{\,2}h_th_t^\top,\\ \widehat V_n^{\mathrm{acq}}&=\widetilde A_n^{-1}\widehat S_n^{\mathrm{acq}}\widetilde A_n^{-1}\longrightarrow V(a_\infty). \end{aligned}\]

For both methods, the acquisition criterion converges to its class-optimal value, no larger than that of uniform acquisition at the same budget, while the acquired fraction converges to \(\rho\):

\[\begin{gathered} a_t\longrightarrow a_\infty,\qquad J_M(a_t)\longrightarrow J_M(a_\infty)\le J_M(\rho)=\rho^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(MV)\quad\text{a.s.},\\ \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^nD_t\longrightarrow\rho\quad\text{a.s.}. \end{gathered}\]
