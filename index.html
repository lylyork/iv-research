Research overview

Statistical inference and budgeted acquisition for online IV regression

Yulin Liu · Advised by Yifan Hu
Updated November 7, 2026 Working draft

Under the guidance of Professor Yifan Hu, I study how online instrumental-variable estimators can quantify uncertainty and use a limited data-acquisition budget more effectively.

I started with the streaming IV algorithms of Chen et al. (2024), developing asymptotic inference and online covariance estimation for their averaged iterates. Inspired by the importance-sampling perspective of Aolaritei et al. (2026), I then formulated an instrument-guided acquisition problem, derived its oracle policy, and developed online procedures with theoretical guarantees under explicit assumptions.

01 / Starting point

The IV problem and its online algorithm

I consider a stream \(W_t=(Z_t,X_t,Y_t)\), where \(Z_t\in\mathbb R^{d_z}\) is an instrument, \(X_t\in\mathbb R^{d_x}\) is an endogenous regressor, and \(Y_t\) is the outcome:

\[\begin{aligned} Y&=X^\top\theta_*+\epsilon_1, &\mathbb E[Z\epsilon_1]&=0,\\ X&=\gamma_*^\top Z+\epsilon_2, &\mathbb E[\epsilon_2\mid Z]&=0. \end{aligned}\]

The errors may be dependent, so ordinary regression need not identify \(\theta_*\). The first stage estimates \(\mathbb E[X\mid Z]=\gamma_*^\top Z\); the second uses this instrument-predictable component to estimate the structural parameter. Define

\[\Sigma_Z=\mathbb E[ZZ^\top],\qquad A=\gamma_*^\top\Sigma_Z\gamma_*\succ0.\]

The population second-stage problem can be written as

\[\begin{gathered} \min_\theta f(\theta),\qquad f(\theta)=\frac12\mathbb E[(Y-Z^\top\gamma_*\theta)^2],\\ \nabla f(\theta)=A(\theta-\theta_*). \end{gathered}\]

My starting point is the one-sample algorithm OTSG-IVaR from Chen et al. It learns both stages using one observation tuple at each arrival:

\[\begin{aligned} \theta_{t+1}&=\theta_t-\alpha_{t+1}\gamma_t^\top Z_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t\theta_t-Y_t),\\ \gamma_{t+1}&=\gamma_t-\beta_{t+1}Z_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t-X_t^\top). \end{aligned}\]

Both updates use the states before arrival \(t\). The algorithm is from the original paper; my work develops inference and acquisition design around it.

The two-sample baseline: TOSG-IVaR

I also study the original two-sample method. It uses a draw \(X_t'\) with the same conditional law as \(X_t\) given \(Z_t\), independent of \((X_t,Y_t)\) given \(Z_t\):

\[\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\alpha_{t+1}(X_t^\top\theta_t-Y_t)X_t'.\]

I establish a diminishing-step mean-square bound supporting averaged-iterate normality and online covariance estimation. Its limiting covariance is \(A^{-1}\mathbb E[\epsilon_1^2X'X'^\top]A^{-1}\), which generally differs from the one-sample covariance below. I focus on OTSG for acquisition because it fits an ordinary one-sample stream.

02 / My first extension

From online estimates to statistical inference

My first question is how to attach valid uncertainty estimates to the online estimator. I study the Polyak–Ruppert average \(\bar\theta_T=T^{-1}\sum_{t=1}^T\theta_t\). Under the report’s moment, rate, and diminishing-step conditions, I obtain

\[\begin{aligned} \sqrt T(\bar\theta_T-\theta_*)&=\frac1{\sqrt T}\sum_{t=1}^T\phi(W_t)+o_p(1),\\ \phi(W)&=A^{-1}\gamma_*^\top Z\epsilon_1,\\ \sqrt T(\bar\theta_T-\theta_*)&\Rightarrow N(0,V),\quad V=\mathbb E[\phi(W)\phi(W)^\top]. \end{aligned}\]

The key cancellation. Estimating the first stage from the same stream creates a leading error term. I show how it cancels the corresponding component of the second-stage noise. The influence function uses the structural residual \(\epsilon_1=Y-X^\top\theta_*\).

This identifies what an online covariance estimator should track. With \(h_t=\gamma_t^\top Z_t\) and \(\widehat\epsilon_t=Y_t-X_t^\top\theta_t\), I maintain

\[\begin{aligned} \widehat A_T&=\gamma_T^\top\left(\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^TZ_tZ_t^\top\right)\gamma_T,\\ \widehat S_T&=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\widehat\epsilon_t^{\,2}h_th_t^\top,\\ \widehat V_T&=\widetilde A_T^{-1}\widehat S_T\widetilde A_T^{-1}\xrightarrow{p}V. \end{aligned}\]

Here \(\widetilde A_T\) thresholds the eigenvalues of \(\widehat A_T\) below a fixed \(0<\tau<\lambda_{\min}(A)\). For coordinates with positive limiting variance, I obtain asymptotic \(1-q\) confidence intervals:

\[\bar\theta_{T,j}\ \pm\ z_{1-q/2}\sqrt{\frac{(\widehat V_T)_{jj}}{T}}.\]

The averaging result requires suitable step schedules and error controls; it does not follow automatically from the original last-iterate rate. Full assumptions and proofs appear in Section 3 of the report.

03 / A new design question

Which observations should I acquire?

The influence function measures an observation’s contribution to uncertainty. Inspired by optimal importance sampling, I ask how to use it in a passive stream: I see \(Z_t\) for free, then decide whether to acquire the costly pair \((X_t,Y_t)\).

\[\begin{gathered} D\mid Z\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(a(Z)),\qquad D\perp(X,Y)\mid Z,\\ \delta\le a(Z)\le1,\qquad \mathbb E[a(Z)]=\rho,\qquad 0<\delta<\rho<1. \end{gathered}\]

The budget \(\rho\) is the expected fraction of acquired pairs. Inverse-probability weighting preserves population expectations:

\[\mathbb E\!\left[\frac D{a(Z)}g(W)\right]=\mathbb E[g(W)].\]

For a fixed policy, the influence-based covariance per raw-arrival normalization is \(V(a)=\mathbb E[\phi(W)\phi(W)^\top/a(Z)]\). I choose a nonzero \(M\succeq0\) to specify which parameter directions matter and formulate

\[\begin{aligned} m_M(z)&=\mathbb E[\phi(W)^\top M\phi(W)\mid Z=z],\\ \min_a\ J_M(a)&=\operatorname{tr}\{MV(a)\}=\mathbb E\!\left[\frac{m_M(Z)}{a(Z)}\right]\\ \text{subject to }&\quad \delta\le a(Z)\le1,\quad\mathbb E[a(Z)]=\rho. \end{aligned}\]

For example, \(M=I\) targets total parameter variance, while \(M=e_je_j^\top\) targets one coordinate. The design criterion follows from the estimator’s influence function.

04 / The population benchmark

A clipped square-root oracle

If the population score were known, I show that the optimal measurable acceptance policy has a simple form. For a positive, integrable score,

\[\begin{gathered} a_M^*(z)=\operatorname{clip}_{[\delta,1]}\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{m_M(z)}{\lambda_M}}\right),\\ \mathbb E[a_M^*(Z)]=\rho. \end{gathered}\]

I calibrate \(\lambda_M>0\) to the budget. The policy allocates more acquisitions where the conditional influence contribution is larger, while enforcing probability bounds.

For intuition, consider the scalar model with \(\mathbb E[Z^2]=1\), \(\gamma_*\ne0\), and \(\sigma^2(z)=\mathbb E[\epsilon_1^2\mid Z=z]\):

\[\begin{gathered} m(z)=\frac{z^2\sigma^2(z)}{\gamma_*^2},\\ a^*(z)\propto |z|\sigma(z)\quad\text{when neither bound is active}. \end{gathered}\]

This exposes the roles of instrument leverage and conditional noise. Its unknown population ingredients are what my online procedures must learn.

05 / Implementable procedures

Learning the estimator and the acquisition policy together

I use a policy \(a_t\) fixed by the observed history before arrival \(t\). After seeing \(Z_t\), I set \(\pi_t=a_t(Z_t)\), draw \(D_t\), and use the same weight \(w_t=D_t/\pi_t\) in both IV updates:

\[\begin{aligned} \theta_{t+1}&=\theta_t-\alpha_{t+1}w_t\gamma_t^\top Z_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t\theta_t-Y_t),\\ \gamma_{t+1}&=\gamma_t-\beta_{t+1}w_tZ_t(Z_t^\top\gamma_t-X_t^\top). \end{aligned}\]

I evaluate these directions only on acceptance; a rejection leaves both states unchanged. I develop two policy learners around this shared recursion.

Direct allocation over fixed strata

I partition instrument space into fixed cells \(C_1,\ldots,C_K\), estimate their probabilities \(\widehat p_{t,j}\) and conditional influence scores \(\widehat m_{t,j}\), and solve the empirical allocation directly:

\[\begin{gathered} a_{t+1,j}=\operatorname{clip}_{[\delta,1]}\sqrt{\widehat m_{t,j}/\lambda_t},\\ \sum_j\widehat p_{t,j}a_{t+1,j}=\rho. \end{gathered}\]

The policy is constant inside each cell, so acceptance preserves the within-cell distribution and supports direct score estimation.

SGD within a feasible policy family

I also optimize a fixed family \(a(z;\eta)\), \(\eta\in\mathcal K\), whose members satisfy the probability bounds and population budget. Projected SGD learns its variance-minimizing member.

The policy-gradient update

Using the pre-arrival geometry and IV states, define the estimated influence score on an acquired observation by

\[\widehat q_t=\widehat\epsilon_t^{\,2}\left\|M^{1/2}\widetilde A_{t-1}^{-1}\gamma_t^\top Z_t\right\|^2.\]

Then update

\[\begin{aligned} \widehat g_t&=-\frac{D_t\widehat q_t}{\pi_t^3}\nabla_\eta a(Z_t;\eta_t),\\ \eta_{t+1}&=\Pi_{\mathcal K}(\eta_t-s_{t+1}\widehat g_t). \end{aligned}\]

On rejection, I set \(\widehat g_t=0\) without observing the score. The cubic inverse probability accounts for sampling and differentiation of the criterion. Only the policy parameter is projected.

06 / What I can establish

Convergence, inference, and the scope of optimality

Under explicit sufficient conditions, I establish IV consistency for predictable acquisition policies with a positive probability floor. Suitable polynomial steps and a deterministic positive policy limit \(a_\infty\) additionally give

\[\begin{aligned} (\theta_t,\gamma_t)&\longrightarrow(\theta_*,\gamma_*)\quad\text{almost surely},\\ \sqrt n(\bar\theta_n-\theta_*)&=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{t=1}^n\frac{D_t}{\pi_t}\phi(W_t)+o_p(1)\\ &\Rightarrow N(0,V(a_\infty)). \end{aligned}\]

The same first-stage cancellation survives adaptive acquisition. My online covariance estimator accounts for squared acquisition weights:

\[\begin{aligned} \widehat S_n^{\mathrm{acq}}&=\frac1n\sum_{t=1}^n\frac{D_t}{\pi_t^2}\widehat\epsilon_t^{\,2}h_th_t^\top,\\ \widehat V_n^{\mathrm{acq}}&=\widetilde A_n^{-1}\widehat S_n^{\mathrm{acq}}\widetilde A_n^{-1}\longrightarrow V(a_\infty). \end{aligned}\]

I prove convergence to the optimum on a fixed partition for direct allocation, and to the class optimum for parametric SGD under strong convexity and regularity. Writing either limiting design as \(a_M^\star\),

\[\begin{gathered} J_M(a_t)\longrightarrow J_M(a_M^\star)\le J_M(\rho)=\rho^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(MV),\\ \frac1n\sum_{t=1}^nD_t\longrightarrow\rho\quad\text{almost surely}. \end{gathered}\]

The comparison uses uniform acquisition at the same budget and the chosen scalar variance criterion. Separately, I show that population-optimal allocations on expanding, refining finite partitions approach the unrestricted oracle:

\[J_M(a_{M,r}^\star)\downarrow J_M(a_M^*).\]
Conditions and boundaries of these results
  • The IV dimensions are fixed, \(\Sigma_Z\succ0\), and \(A\succ0\). The adaptive analysis uses the report’s finite composite fourth-moment condition and a fixed floor \(\pi_t\ge\delta>0\).
  • Adaptive normality uses \(\alpha_t\propto t^{-a}\), \(\beta_t\propto t^{-b}\), with \(1/2<a,b<1\) and \(b>2a-1\), together with a deterministic policy limit.
  • Parametric convergence uses a fixed feasible family, a compact convex parameter set, a bounded policy gradient, a strongly convex criterion, and policy-step exponent in \((1/2,1)\).
  • Class optimality need not equal the unrestricted oracle. Partition refinement compares population optima; the online theorem keeps the partition fixed.
  • The budget is an expected sampling proportion, and the acquired fraction converges to it. I do not impose a hard sample-count budget or assert a theorem for stopping exactly at a prescribed acquisition count.
  • The comparison does not assert finite-sample error improvement or covariance improvement in every direction. Selective acquisition also cannot improve on full observation at the same raw-arrival horizon.

Sections 5–6 and the appendices give the full conditions and proofs. Finite-sample coverage and learned-policy performance require separate empirical evaluation.

This project has led me from uncertainty quantification for an existing estimator to a design problem in which estimation, inference, and acquisition must remain compatible while learning from the same stream.
